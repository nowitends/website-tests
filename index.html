01 / GENERAL THEORY

What does the determinant mean?

The determinant is a number attached to a square matrix. In three dimensions, it measures the signed volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the row vectors. Its magnitude measures volume; its sign tracks orientation.

|A|

Magnitude = volume

For a 3 × 3 matrix, the absolute value |det A| equals the volume spanned by its three row vectors. If the vectors are coplanar, the volume is zero.

Volume = |det A| ≥ 0
↕

Swapping changes sign

A determinant is alternating in its rows. Exchanging any two rows reverses the orientation, while the volume stays exactly the same.

det(swapi,j A) = −det A
Σ

Cofactor expansion

Expand along any row: multiply each entry by its 2 × 2 minor and the appropriate checkerboard sign. All row expansions produce the same determinant.

det A = Σj (−1)i+j aij Mij

Three essential row operations

Exchange rows: Ri ↔ Rjdet → −det
Scale a row: Ri → kRidet → k·det
Add another row: Ri → Ri + kRjdet unchanged
Two identical rows: Ri = Rjdet = 0

Cofactor sign pattern

+−+−+−+−+

Each minor Mij is the determinant of the 2 × 2 matrix left after deleting row i and column j.

Solve it, one step at a time.

Compute det A, exchange the first two rows, predict det B from the theorem, and verify by direct calculation. Click Next step to animate the argument.

The determinant is found by expanding along the first row.
Show the complete written solution and Sarrus verification

1. Compute det A by expanding across row 1.

det A = 1(3·1 − 1·1) − 2(0·1 − 1·2) + 0
= 1·2 − 2·(−2)
= 2 + 4 = 6

2. Swap rows 1 and 2. Now B = [[0, 3, 1], [1, 2, 0], [2, 1, 1]]. Prediction: det B = −det A = −6.

3. Verify det B by expanding across row 1.

det B = 0 − 3(1·1 − 0·2) + 1(1·1 − 2·2)
= −3·1 + 1·(−3)
= −6

4. Sarrus check for A: downward products 3 + 4 + 0 = 7, upward products 0 + 1 + 0 = 1. Thus det A = 7 − 1 = 6.

Why does the sign flip? Think of the three rows as an ordered basis. Swapping two vectors reverses the handedness of that ordered basis. The parallelepiped is geometrically identical, but its orientation is opposite. If det = 0, its 3D volume collapses to zero.
